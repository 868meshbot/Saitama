# The apps explained

Every app in Saitama, in plain English: what it's for and why you'd want it.

**Both** = on the T-Deck and the Cardputer. **T-Deck** = T-Deck only.

## Talking to people

### Chat (both)
**What it is:** your messages, like a text-message app on a phone.

**Why you'd use it:** to talk. Group chats are called **channels**. Everyone can
read **Public**; you can make private channels for your family, friends or club. You
can also send a **direct message** to one person only. Direct messages are
**verified**: a green padlock shows the message really came from that person and
wasn't faked.

*Example: you're walking in the hills with friends, out of phone signal. You make a
channel called "Walk", and everyone can see "I'm at the car park" and "Waiting at the
top".*

### Contacts (both)
**What it is:** your address book of people on the mesh.

**Why you'd use it:** to message someone directly, star your favourites, see when
you last heard from them, and block anyone causing trouble. It's also how you share
your own details by QR code.

### Heard (both)
**What it is:** a live list of every device your radio has picked up since you
switched it on, with how strong their signal was.

**Why you'd use it:** to see who's around right now. If you've just arrived
somewhere, Heard tells you at a glance whether anyone's in range. You can save
anyone in it as a contact.

## Looking after the network

### Repeaters (both)
**What it is:** a list of the repeaters near you. Repeaters are the always-on radios
that pass messages along.

**Why you'd use it:** to see which repeaters you can reach and how good the link is.
If you run a repeater, you can log in to it from here: check its battery, see who it
can hear, change its password, all without climbing up to it with a laptop.

### Finder (both)
**What it is:** a quick "who can hear me right now?" scan. It only counts devices that
hear you **directly**, with no repeater in between.

**Why you'd use it:** to find a good spot. Move around, run Finder again, and see if
more devices hear you. Great for choosing where to put a repeater, or checking your
antenna is working.

### Regions (both)
**What it is:** asks nearby repeaters which **regions** they cover, and lets you save
them.

**Why you'd use it:** some busy meshes only pass on messages marked for their area
(to stop messages flooding the whole country). If your messages aren't getting out,
Regions tells you which area tag to use. See [Regions and scopes](Regions-and-Scopes).

### Signal (both)
**What it is:** the "health check" for your radio: how strong the last signal was,
how noisy the airwaves are, and how many messages have gone in and out.

**Why you'd use it:** when something seems wrong. If the received count isn't going
up, you're not hearing anything. If the noise is high, something nearby is
interfering.

### Advertise (T-Deck) / Settings > Advert (Cardputer)
**What it is:** sends out a short "Hello, I'm here" announcement, called an
**advert**.

**Why you'd use it:** other people can only message you once they've heard your
advert. Send one when you arrive somewhere new. You can also have it sent
automatically every few hours.

### Trace (T-Deck)
**What it is:** follows the path a message takes, hop by hop, and shows the signal
at each step.

**Why you'd use it:** to find the weak link. If messages to someone keep failing,
Trace shows which repeater on the way is struggling.

## Where am I?

### Map (T-Deck)
**What it is:** a proper map, stored on the SD card, so it works with no internet.
People who share their location appear on it.

**Why you'd use it:** to see where your friends and nearby repeaters are, or just to
find your way when your phone has no signal.

### GPS (both)
**What it is:** shows your position, the time, and how many satellites the GPS can
see. On the T-Deck it draws a sky map of the satellites.

**Why you'd use it:** to check you've got a fix before sharing your location, or to
read your exact coordinates out to someone.

## Tools for the curious (T-Deck)

These are for people who like to see what's happening on the airwaves. You don't
need any of them for everyday messaging.

| App | In plain English | Why it's useful |
|---|---|---|
| **ChanScan** | Listens on the channels next to yours and shows how busy each one is. | Spot interference, or find a quieter frequency. |
| **Spectrum** | A picture of radio activity across the band, like a graphic equaliser. | See signals and interference you can't hear. |
| **SigGen** | Sends a test signal. | Testing antennas and other radios. |
| **PCAP** | Records the raw messages your radio hears to the SD card. | For experts studying how the network behaves. |
| **BT Foxhunt** | Beeps faster as you get closer to a chosen Bluetooth device. | A "hot and cold" game for finding a lost tracker, or a fun group hunt. |
| **Power** | Battery history and how long it should last. | Know whether you'll make it through the day. |
